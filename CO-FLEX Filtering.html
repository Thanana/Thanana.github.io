Scores \(\Phi_t(s)\) → Spread \(\bar\varsigma\) → Design region \(\Delta > 2\bar\varsigma\) → Margin rule \(\delta\) → Structure \(s_{t+1}\)

The design region \(\Delta > 2\bar\varsigma\) is necessary and sufficient for score descent and finite switching. A data-dependent margin tracks \(\bar\varsigma\) online — no offline calibration required.

Cognitive Flexibility (CF) selects, at each step, the latent structure that minimises an innovation-based predictive score, guaranteeing finite switching and score descent when scores are exact. In practice the switching rate also depends on how the margin threshold \(\delta\) is chosen relative to the score spread, yet no design principle has connected the two.

What Is the Problem?

Existing CF theory guarantees structural descent and finite switching under exact scores, but gives no guidance on choosing \(\delta\). Too small a margin admits spurious switches driven by score variation; too large a margin slows recovery after a genuine regime change. The gap between the margin and the score spread is the quantity that actually governs the switching rate, yet it has never been formalised.

Design Region and Main Results

  • Theorem 4 (Design region): \(\Delta > 2\bar\varsigma\) is both necessary and sufficient for the descent and finite-switching guarantees to hold with high probability, where \(\Delta\) is the margin and \(\bar\varsigma\) the spread of settled-step scores.
  • Theorem 5 (Optimality): no selector consistently beats the threshold rule inside the design region.
  • Corollary 1 (Data-dependent margin): a margin that tracks \(\bar\varsigma\) online from the settled non-good steps \(\mathcal{X}_\delta\) stays inside the design region without offline calibration.
  • Label-free margin \(\hat\varsigma^{(W)}_t\): estimated from \(W\)-step score means — no regime labels required.
  • IMM-d design rule: applies the same principle to \(-\log\) IMM mode probabilities, bringing the design region to soft-output particle filters.

Core Insight

The margin must exceed twice the score spread to protect the switching guarantees. A data-dependent margin that tracks this spread online matches offline-calibrated performance — without requiring labelled regime data or a held-out calibration set.

Robust Cognitive–Flexible Filtering Under Noisy Innovation Scores

IEEE Signal Processing Letters, vol. 33, pp. 4036–4040, 2026  ·  DOI: 10.1109/LSP.2026.3737379

▶ Published paper: IEEE Xplore
Julia source code:
  • thanana.github.io — code and reproducibility materials

Noisy scores \(\hat\Phi_t(s)\) → Noise envelope \(\varepsilon_t^{\mathrm{env}}\) → Adaptive margin \(\delta_t\) → Switch if \(\hat\Phi_t(s_t)-\hat\Phi_t(\hat s_t) > \delta_t\) → Structure \(s_{t+1}\)

A margin \(\delta > 2\bar\varepsilon\) recovers score descent, finite expected switching, and almost-sure non-chattering despite bounded score noise. A batch-split estimator tracks the noise envelope online at no extra Monte Carlo cost.

Cognitive Flexibility (CF) assumes exact innovation scores; particle filters corrupt them with bounded, conditionally zero-mean noise. We introduce a margin-based switching rule calibrated against the noise bound \(\bar\varepsilon\) and prove all three noiseless-style guarantees are recovered. The fixed margin is then replaced by fully online \(\mathcal{I}_t\)-adapted sequences, and all three guarantees persist whenever the effective margin \(\delta_t - 2\varepsilon_t\) stays above a floor.

Main Results

  • Theorem 1 (Score descent): under \(\delta > 2\bar\varepsilon\), each switch strictly decreases the true score.
  • Theorem 2 (Finite expected switching): \(\mathbb{E}[N_T] \le C/(\delta-2\bar\varepsilon)\).
  • Theorem 3 (Almost-sure non-chattering): the switching sequence is eventually non-chattering a.s.
  • Theorem 4 (Adaptive margin): all three guarantees survive time-varying \(\varepsilon_t,\,\delta_t\) provided \(\delta_t - 2\varepsilon_t \ge m > 0\).
  • Batch-split estimator: reuses the existing particle budget; needs no stationary validation window.

Core Insight

A margin that exceeds twice the noise bound is both necessary and sufficient to recover the noiseless CF guarantees under particle-filter score corruption. Adapting the margin online to a running noise-envelope estimate closes the loop without offline calibration.
