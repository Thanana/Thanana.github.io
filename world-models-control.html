World Models and Control

Learning to infer, predict, and control under uncertainty.

My research connects Bayesian state estimation, stochastic world models, and adaptive control. I study how a robot can maintain a useful belief about its environment, revise that belief when the model is mismatched, and use it for downstream decisions.

The work develops from structural belief adaptation through reliable latent modeling and controller-aware inference, toward compliant robotic interaction.

Infer: estimate hidden states from incomplete observations.
Adapt: change the representation when a fixed model becomes restrictive.
Assess: identify when a latent belief supports a physical certificate.
Control: relate belief corrections to the controller's input tolerances.

Seeing Beyond Vision: Stochastic World Models

A world model describes how a system evolves and how observations arise. Under partial observability, a robot must reason about hidden states and possible futures, rather than rely only on the current image.

Deep Stochastic State-Space Models (Deep SSSM) combine a probabilistic encoder, stochastic latent transitions, and variational inference. The aim is to carry uncertainty from perception into prediction, providing a foundation for planning under noise and occlusion.

Research Threads and Selected Work

1. Structural Belief Adaptation

What if parameter tuning cannot repair the model mismatch?

Cognitive Flexibility selects among latent structures using innovation-based predictive evidence, while preserving the Bayesian filtering recursion. The analysis separates well-posedness, the selection mechanism, and its consequences under the stated conditions.

Study: Cognitive Flexibility as a Latent Structural Operator for Bayesian State Estimation

2. Robust Adaptation under Noisy Evidence

When does a score difference justify changing the model?

Robust CF introduces switching margins that account for noisy innovation scores. A batch-split estimator uses existing particles to inform online margin calibration as the noise scale changes. The guarantees depend on the stated score-noise and effective-margin conditions.

Study: Robust Cognitive-Flexible Filtering under Noisy Innovation Scores

3. Reliable Latent Prediction

Can a learned belief remain useful when perception disappears?

Deep SSSM studies stochastic latent dynamics and uncertainty propagation for occlusion-robust robotic prediction. A variational framework connects representation learning and prediction, laying a modeling foundation for future predictive and Bayesian model predictive control.

Study: Deep Stochastic State-Space Modeling for Occlusion-Robust Robotic Manipulation

4. Physical-Latent Certification

When can a decoded latent belief support a physical safety claim?

SafeCF-SSM combines channel-wise decoder error and belief uncertainty in a realizability test. Predictive surprise regulates both adaptation and safety margins. Each constrained channel is certified under the stated assumptions or explicitly declared uncertifiable under the given codec.

In the reported quadrotor rotor-failure simulation, attitude channels remain certifiable while altitude is declared uncertifiable. The contribution makes the physical-latent gap checkable; it does not assume that the gap disappears.

Study: SafeCF-SSM: Certifying, Not Closing, the Physical-Latent Problem in Adaptive Latent-Space Control

5. Controller-Aware Belief Correction

How far may a neural observer move its belief before the MPC plan changes too much?

Neural Belief MPC derives separate correction and same-context adaptation budgets from prescribed input tolerances within a known affine MPC cell. It examines why constraints on the current input can miss changes later in the predicted plan.

These certificates bound belief and input changes under the stated assumptions. Plan consistency alone does not establish true-state accuracy, nonlinear safety, or recursive feasibility.

Study: Neural Belief Corrections for MPC: Plan-Consistent Scalar Budgets versus Myopic Directional Constraints

6. Model Switching and Recovery

How should sample size, sample refreshes, and hysteresis be designed together?

Hysteretic Selection separates changes in the evidence from changes caused by refreshing Monte Carlo samples. Recorded-score accounts relate switching and recovery to sample size, refresh rate, and margin. A key distinction is that fewer switches can coexist with persistent wrong decisions.

Study: Switching and Recovery Guarantees for Hysteretic Model Selection under Monte Carlo Likelihood Refreshes

Toward Compliant Intelligence

The longer-term direction connects uncertainty-aware tactile perception, stochastic belief, structural adaptation, and adaptive decision making in compliant robotic systems:

Feel -> Believe -> Adapt -> Decide -> Interact

Co-Flex, a direction in preparation, asks when and how often a model structure may switch within a closed loop. Hysteretic Selection is a related switching study and remains a separate work.

Physical embodiment builds on earlier compliant pneumatic-artificial-muscle control and connects to Tactile Intelligence. Integrating the full belief-to-interaction pipeline remains a research objective; the studies above address its individual components.
