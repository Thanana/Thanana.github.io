Switching and Recovery Guarantees for Hysteretic Model Selection under Monte Carlo Likelihood Refreshes


Preparing for submitting to IEEE Control Systems Letters, October 2026

Monte Carlo likelihood → Sample bank \((M,\ \chi)\) → Hysteretic selector \(\delta\) → Observer / controller

Redrawing Monte Carlo samples can switch the selected model even when the evidence moves slowly; reusing one bank stops this but can lock in a wrong decision. Sample size, refresh rate and margin must be designed together — and fewer switches can mean persistent wrong decisions.

 

(a) Selection loop with design variables \(M,\ \chi,\ \delta\). (b) Schematic for two models: query motion moves the evaluated gap smoothly, refreshes make it jump, and crossing to the far side of the band \(\pm\delta\) is a switch. Refreshes alone cause a wrong switch and its reversal.

What Is the Problem?

A supervisor that selects one model from a finite dictionary must respond to regime changes while limiting reconfiguration. A hard selector is needed when one model drives a downstream action, such as gain scheduling, controller reconfiguration or allocating computation to a single filter. With Monte Carlo likelihoods, every refresh of the sample bank perturbs the evidence and can trigger switches by itself; reusing one bank removes this effect but can lock in a wrong decision.

Main Results

  • Recorded-score account (Theorems 1–2). On every path, switches are bounded by the initial potential plus the upward variation of the recorded scores, divided by the margin; an exact identity counts corrections. No accuracy certificate is needed.

  • Refresh-aware design (Theorem 3). Query motion and bank refreshes enter the switch budget separately, giving an explicit margin window that guarantees a prescribed switching density and immediate correction when the true score gap exceeds a threshold.

  • Uniform concentration (Lemma 2). Hoeffding bounds on likelihood ratios, uniform over a covering of the query region and valid for sample-dependent queries, turn the window into a sample size for continuous predictive laws with bounded kernels.

  • Observer consequence (Corollary 2). A selected-model observer inherits an average-dwell-time bound, conditional on the interval count.

Core Insight

Fewer switches can mean persistent wrong decisions. With four samples per reused bank, the exact failure probability per regime change is 5/16 = 31.25%, mostly from a hysteresis dead zone rather than rank reversal; the observed rate is 32.5 ± 1.7%.

Scope

The certificates are sufficient, not necessary, and hold on a high-probability accuracy event. The Gaussian tracking study measures the conservatism of the recorded-score accounts; Lemma 2 is not evaluated for that tracker.
