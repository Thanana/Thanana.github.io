Robust Cognitive-Flexible Filtering under Noisy Innovation Scores

Accepted in IEEE Signal Processing Letters (SPL)

Noisy Scores \(\hat{\Phi}_t(s)\) → Batch-Split Spread \(\hat{\varepsilon}_t\) → Adaptive Margin \(\delta_t\) → Robust CF Update \(s_{t+1}\)

A batch-split estimator bounds conditionally zero-mean particle noise online with zero overhead, driving an adaptive margin \(\delta_t\) that prevents spurious switching while guaranteeing structural descent.

Graphical Abstract

Adaptive Margin Architecture: A batch-split estimator extracts score noise variance \(\hat{\varepsilon}_t\) from existing particles with zero overhead. This dynamically scales an adaptive margin \(\delta_t\), structurally preventing noise-induced spurious switching while rigorously preserving descent and finite-switching guarantees under nonstationary regimes.

Cognitive Flexibility (CF) resolves structural mismatch in Bayesian filtering by selecting, at each step, the structure minimizing an innovation-based predictive score. However, existing theory assumes exact scores, whereas practical particle filters corrupt them with bounded, conditionally zero-mean noise. Without protection, this noise induces spurious switching — degrading accuracy, inflating computational cost, and invalidating noiseless guarantees.

The Solution: Adaptive Margin via Batch-Split Estimator

We introduce a margin-based switching rule where a structural transition only occurs if the noisy score advantage of the challenger exceeds \(\delta_t\). To avoid relying on an offline, fixed worst-case bound, we developed a zero-overhead online calibration scheme:

  • Batch-Split Estimator: Partitions the \(N_p\) particles into \(B\) disjoint batches. The spread among these batches directly measures the approximation noise \(\hat{\varepsilon}_t\) without requiring any additional Monte Carlo simulations.
  • Peak-Hold Envelope: Passes \(\hat{\varepsilon}_t\) through a geometric envelope \(\bar{\varepsilon}_t = \max\{\hat{\varepsilon}_t, \lambda\bar{\varepsilon}_{t-1}\}\) to ensure it acts as a robust upper bound.
  • Adaptive Margin \(\delta_t\): Scales directly with the envelope (\(\delta_t = \max\{(2+\eta)\bar{\varepsilon}_{t-1}, \delta_{\min}\}\)), ensuring the effective margin \(\delta_t - 2\varepsilon_t\) remains strictly positive.

Main Theoretical Guarantees

Replacing the static bound with our \(\mathcal{I}_t\)-adapted sequence, we formally prove that:

  • Expected Score Descent (Theorem 2): \(\mathbb{E}[\Phi_{t}(s_{t+1})\mid\mathcal{I}_{t}] \leq \mathbb{E}[\Phi_{t}(s_{t})\mid\mathcal{I}_{t}]\)
  • Finite Expected Switching (Theorem 3): \(\mathbb{E}[N_T]\) is strictly bounded by cumulative score suboptimality scaled by the effective margin.
  • Non-Chattering a.s. (Theorem 4): Structure selection stabilizes almost surely as the system converges.

Simulation Study: Li-Jilkov CV/CT Radar Tracking

Does the adaptive margin successfully suppress noise-induced false alarms without requiring manual tuning or compromising responsiveness?

We validate the architecture on the classic Li-Jilkov manoeuvring target benchmark (Constant Velocity to Coordinated Turn). Crucially, noise is not artificially injected. Scores are genuine particle-filter estimates, meaning \(\epsilon_t(s)\) represents real Monte Carlo error. We impose a nonstationary budget schedule (\(N_p=4000\) dropping to \(N_p=60\)) to mimic a radar resource manager. This sudden drop in particles causes the noise scale to spike, rigorously testing the adaptive margin exactly when scores are least reliable.

By explicitly adapting to the nonstationary noise envelope, the proposed rule cuts false alarms tenfold and matches the offline oracle calibration without requiring any manual tuning in deployment.
