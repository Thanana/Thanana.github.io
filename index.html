Research Vision

Learning without guaranteed bounds is a liability. My vision is to engineer autonomous systems — from embodied robots to cyber-physical architectures — that rigorously feel, believe, adapt, and decide in open environments. Every adaptation in the system's belief state must be mathematically certified against the physical tolerances of the actions it drives. I define this paradigm as Compliant Intelligence: achieving robust stability by dynamically embracing uncertainty rather than rigidly resisting it.

Feel → Believe → Adapt → Decide → Interact

Scientific Research Philosophy

One object, one question. I study the belief a learning-enabled robot acts on, and ask how far it may change — switch, be corrected, adapt — with guarantees stated in the tolerances of the decision it drives.

  • When to switch. Certified structure switching: how often a robot may change its model of the world, and how fast it must recover, when the evidence itself is noisy.

  • How far to correct. Controller-aware belief budgets: limits on what a neural observer may change, set directly by the input tolerances of MPC.

  • When to trust. Reliability of learned latent belief: when quantities decoded from it can be used for safety, especially when perception drops out.

  • Where it lands. Compliant robots that touch: tactile perception and soft actuation as the physical test of these guarantees.

“Challenge. Change. Impact.”
