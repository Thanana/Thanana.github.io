Biography

Dr. Thanana Nuchkrua is a researcher in control and intelligent systems, with a background in soft actuation, nonlinear and adaptive control. His research has evolved from controlling physical motion and interaction to developing Bayesian state estimation and stochastic world models for learning-enabled control. His work spans robotics and cyber-physical systems, with broader interests in understanding and controlling natural and engineered dynamical systems.

At the heart of his work is a question: what should a system change in its beliefs, and how far, so that those beliefs remain useful for control? This question connects his foundations in soft actuation and nonlinear robot control with his recent studies of latent dynamics, structural belief adaptation, and controller-aware inference.

His current research on World Models and Control examines how internal representations can evolve with new evidence while meeting the demands of downstream control. His studies address adaptation of latent structures, robustness to noisy predictive evidence, and uncertainty propagation under partial observability, alongside the conditions under which belief corrections remain compatible with a controller's requirements.

Education and International Research Experience

He earned his Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering in 2015 and has held appointments as a Postdoctoral Researcher, Research Associate (Faculty), and Visiting Scholar across National Chung Cheng University (Taiwan), Huazhong University of Science and Technology (China), Guangdong Technion–Israel Institute of Technology (China), and King Mongkut's University of Technology Thonburi (Thailand).
